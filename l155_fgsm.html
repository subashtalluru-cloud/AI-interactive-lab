Adversarial examples: many tiny, aligned nudges

The fast gradient sign method moves every input feature by the same small amount ε, each in whichever direction hurts the prediction most. No single change is noticeable; together they can flip the answer.

2 · The same arithmetic with many pixels

What to notice

In the book’s example, ε = 0.15 lowers the logit by 0.15 × (2 + 3 + 1) = 0.9: from 0.8 (p = 0.69, positive) to −0.10 (p = 0.475, negative).

In the image panel the logit falls by ε times the sum of all weight magnitudes, which grows with the number of pixels. A change of one grey level per pixel — invisible — moves a 64 × 64 classifier’s logit far more than a 4 × 4 one’s. This linear accumulation in high dimensions is a main reason adversarial examples exist (Goodfellow et al.), and why defences must be tested against deliberate attack, not only random noise (Chapter 155).
