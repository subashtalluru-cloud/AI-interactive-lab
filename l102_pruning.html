Pruning: which weights can go?

Remove the smallest weights and the output barely changes — usually. But a small weight on a large input matters more than its size suggests. Compare magnitude pruning with activation-aware pruning.

What to notice

w = (0.9, −0.05, 0.4, 0.02, −0.7, 0.1), x = (1, 2, 0.5, 1.5, 1, 0.8): output 0.41. Removing the three smallest weights leaves 0.40 — half the weights, 0.01 of change (Math Box 102.1).

Raise x₂ to 20. The “small” weight −0.05 now contributes −1.0, the largest term. Magnitude pruning still removes it and the output jumps; activation-aware pruning (the idea behind Wanda) keeps it. Real pruning is followed by fine-tuning to recover, and structured pruning removes whole units so hardware actually runs faster (Chapter 102).
