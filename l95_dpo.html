DPO on one preference pair

Direct preference optimization skips the separate reward model. It compares how much the current model has raised the preferred answer, and lowered the dispreferred one, relative to a frozen reference — and pushes that margin up.

What to notice

Log-ratios +2 (preferred) and −1 (dispreferred): margin 3, β × margin 0.3, implied preference σ(0.3) = 0.574, loss 0.554. At the start, 0.693; at margin 10, 0.313 (Math Box 95.1).

Only the difference of log-ratios matters, so DPO can reduce the loss by lowering the dispreferred answer’s probability rather than raising the preferred one — one reason DPO-trained models sometimes lose fluency. β plays the role of the KL leash in RLHF (Chapters 94–95).
