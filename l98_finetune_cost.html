What a fine-tuning job costs

Rough arithmetic before starting: how many tokens, how many steps, how many FLOPs and how much memory. For most clinical adaptation jobs, compute is small; memory is the binding constraint.

What to notice

The book’s job — 2,000 examples of 600 + 150 tokens, 3 epochs, 7B model — is about 1.9 × 10¹⁷ FLOPs: roughly 25 minutes on one accelerator, and about one two-hundred-thousandth of pretraining. 375 optimizer steps.

Switch to full fine-tuning at 70B and memory, not time, becomes the problem: weights, gradients and optimizer states come to roughly 16 bytes per parameter. LoRA keeps gradients and optimizer states only for the adapter; QLoRA also stores the frozen base in 4 bits (Chapters 98–100). For LoRA the lab uses 4ND rather than 6ND, a rough allowance for skipping most weight-gradient work; real savings vary with implementation. The memory figures are rules of thumb that ignore activations, which depend on sequence length and batch size.
