Is the benchmark score partly memory?

Score a model on a published benchmark, then on fresh problems matched in topic and difficulty that it cannot have seen. A gap much larger than chance suggests some of the published score came from having met those problems — or near-copies — in training.

What to notice

80% against 68% on 1,000 problems each: the standard error of the difference is about 0.02 (0.019 unrounded), so the 12-point gap is about 6 standard errors — far beyond chance (Math Box 151.1).

Shrink the sets to 100 problems and the same gap is just under 2 standard errors (1.96) — suggestive, not conclusive. A significant gap still has two explanations: contamination, or fresh problems that are harder in some unnoticed way. Checking that models trained before the benchmark existed score the same on both sets helps separate them (Chapter 151).
