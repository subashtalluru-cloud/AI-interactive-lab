Gradient descent on a bowl

The same four numbers as Chapter 15: x = 1, 2, 3, 4 and y = 2, 3, 5, 6. Watch the line move, the residual springs shorten, and the loss fall — then break it by raising the learning rate.

Three things to try

Leave η at 0.05 and press “Run 50”. The loss falls 18.5 → 0.55 → 0.064 in the first three steps, exactly as computed in Chapter 15, then creeps to 0.05 as the slope flattens near the bottom.

Raise η to about 0.11 and step. The path zig-zags across the valley: the loss is far more sensitive to w than to b, so the steepest-descent direction does not point at the minimum. Feature scaling would round the contours out.

Raise η past about 0.12 and step a few times. The loss increases: each step overshoots the bottom by more than it started with, and the parameters diverge. The threshold is 2 divided by the largest curvature of the loss (here about 16.7), so η ≈ 0.12. This is the same arithmetic that makes a too-large learning rate ruin a real training run.
