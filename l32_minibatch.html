Mini-batch gradients: cheap, noisy, unbiased

The true gradient averages over every training example. A mini-batch estimates it from a few. Each estimate points roughly the right way; the average of many is exactly right; the noise shrinks as 1/√B.

What to notice

Gradients −4, −1, +2, −5; true mean −2. Batches of two give values like −1, −3 or −4.5: all downhill, by varying amounts (Math Box 32.1).

On the large dataset, every fourfold increase in batch size halves the spread. Past a point, bigger batches buy little accuracy per unit of compute — and a bit of noise may even help, nudging training toward flatter, better-generalizing minima [WELL SUPPORTED, mechanisms debated] (Chapters 27, 32).
