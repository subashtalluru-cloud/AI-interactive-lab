Reading a scaling law

Loss falls as a power of model size: L = E + A / N^α. On log–log axes the reducible part is a straight line. Fit it on small models, extrapolate to a large one — and see how uncertainty and the loss floor shape the prediction.

What to notice

With α = 0.076, every tenfold increase in parameters multiplies loss by 10^−0.076 ≈ 0.84 — the same 16% whether going from 1 to 10 billion or from 100 billion to 1 trillion, at a hundred times the cost.

Add a loss floor E and fit a pure power law anyway: the line keeps falling while the true curve flattens, so the extrapolation is too optimistic — and the uncertainty band, which only reflects noise in the measurements, confidently excludes the true value. Error bars cannot warn you that the model’s form is wrong. Fit only up to 10⁷ and the band at 10¹² widens. Scaling laws were valuable because they were smooth enough to predict expensive runs from cheap ones — within the range where the form still holds (Chapter 85).
