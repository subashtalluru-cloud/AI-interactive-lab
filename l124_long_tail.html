Why confident errors cluster in the long tail

A model is more reliable about facts it saw often in training. Most clinical questions are about common facts — but the errors come disproportionately from the rare ones, and the answers sound equally fluent either way.

The accuracy curve is a stylized illustration from Chapter 124, not a measured law.

What to notice

With P(correct) = 1 − 0.5·e^(−n/20): n = 10 → 0.70; n = 50 → 0.96; n = 200 → 1.00. A 70/20/10 mix of common, uncommon and rare questions gives 0.89 overall, and the rare 10% supply almost half the errors (0.05 of 0.11) (Math Box 124.1).

Overall accuracy says little about the questions a specialist actually needs help with. Retrieval (Chapter 125 onward) helps precisely here: it gives the model the rare fact at the moment it is needed instead of relying on how often it was seen.
