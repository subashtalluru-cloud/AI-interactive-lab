Padding, stride and the size of a feature map

Four dials set how big a convolution’s output is. The formula is just counting: how many places can the kernel sit, stepping s at a time, on an input padded by p on each side?

What to notice

With a 5 × 5 input and 3 × 3 kernel: no padding → 3; padding 1 → 5 (same size); padding 1 and stride 2 → 3, a downsampled map (Math Box 46.1).

Padding lets edge pixels be seen as often as central ones and keeps sizes steady through many layers. Stride and pooling shrink the map, letting later layers see a larger area of the image for the same cost — the growing receptive field of Chapter 46. The price is resolution: every halving is a chance to lose a lesion only a few pixels wide.
