Positional encoding: clock hands for word order

Attention alone sees a set, not a sequence. Sinusoidal encodings give each position a pattern of sine and cosine waves at many speeds — fast hands to tell neighbours apart, slow hands to tell distant positions apart.

What to notice

With d = 4: position 1 → (0.841, 0.540, 0.010, 1.000); position 2 → (0.909, −0.416, 0.020, 1.000). The fast pair changes a lot between neighbours; the slow pair barely moves (Math Box 71.1).

Because shifting position by k is a fixed rotation of each (sin, cos) pair, the encoding of p + k is a linear function of the encoding of p — useful for “the word two back” patterns. Modern models mostly use RoPE, which applies such rotations directly to queries and keys (Chapter 71).
