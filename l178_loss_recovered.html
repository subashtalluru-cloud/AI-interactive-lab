How much of the computation does an explanation capture?

Replace a model’s internal activations with a sparse autoencoder’s reconstruction and see how much worse the model gets. “Loss recovered” sounds reassuring; perplexity shows what the remainder costs.

What to notice

Loss 2.00 → 2.30 with the SAE, 8.00 with zeros: (8.00 − 2.30)/(8.00 − 2.00) = 95% of the loss recovered — yet perplexity rises from e² ≈ 7.4 to e^2.3 ≈ 10.0 (Math Box 178.1).

The unexplained residual — sometimes called “dark matter” — can hold exactly the computation one most wants to understand. A 95% figure measures coverage against a very poor baseline, not faithfulness to the model (Chapters 83, 160, 178).
