One-shot question or structured workflow?

A departmental guideline needs, say, 30 factual claims. Ask once and edit, or build a workflow with source extraction and checking steps? The workflow costs more time; the question is what it buys in errors that nobody notices.

All numbers are illustrative defaults. The times are those of Math Box 147.1; the error and catch rates are assumptions to vary, not measurements.

What to notice

One-shot: about 22 minutes. Workflow: about 95. Unaided: 4–6 hours (Math Box 147.1). With the default assumptions, the one-shot version leaves about 0.9 undetected errors in the document; the workflow about 0.09. The break-even is about 90 minutes per undetected error.

Set the cost of an error near zero — a draft for your own eyes — and one-shot wins. Raise it — a guideline other clinicians will follow — and the workflow’s extra 73 minutes are cheap. The workflow’s real advantage is not only fewer errors but traceability: each claim has a quoted source a reviewer can check (Chapters 138, 147).
