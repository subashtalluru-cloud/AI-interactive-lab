Memory at inference: cache, state, or retrieval

A transformer remembers every past token in its key–value cache, which grows with the context. A state-space model compresses the past into a fixed-size state. A hybrid keeps a few attention layers. Retrieval keeps only what it fetches. Slide the context length and compare.

What to notice

32 layers × 1,024 width × 2 (K and V) × 2 bytes = 131 KB per token: 13 GB at 100,000 tokens, 131 GB at a million. The SSM state stays at about 4 MB; the 4-attention-layer hybrid at 16 KB per token; retrieval of 5,000 tokens at under 1 GB (Math Boxes 167.1, 177.1).

Memory is only half the comparison. The fixed state is lossy — exact recall of a detail from far back is where SSMs are weakest — and retrieval fails whenever it misses what mattered (Chapter 127). The hybrid’s bet is a small exact memory plus a cheap compressed one (Chapter 177). Configurations here are illustrative; the linear-versus-constant scaling is general.
