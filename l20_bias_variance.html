Bias and variance, seen directly

Draw a small training set from a curved relationship with noise, fit a polynomial, and repeat twenty times. Simple models are consistently wrong; flexible ones are wrong differently every time.

What to notice

Degree 0–1: the twenty curves agree with each other but miss the truth — high bias. Degree 7–9 with 12 points: the curves swing wildly between samples — high variance. The best test error sits in between.

At degree 5 and above with only 12 points, the error numbers explode into the hundreds or thousands: between and beyond the training points the curves swing far from anything in the data. The error chart is capped so the low end stays readable. Training error, meanwhile, keeps falling — the clearest possible picture of why training error alone cannot tell you whether a model will generalize.

Raise the number of training points: variance shrinks and higher degrees become safe. Raise λ: the flexible models calm down. Both are Chapter 20 and 43's remedies — more data, or regularization. The noise floor never moves; no model can predict the noise.
