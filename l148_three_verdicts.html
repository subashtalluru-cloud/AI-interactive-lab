One model, three verdicts

The same diabetic-retinopathy model is “excellent” on a balanced benchmark, disappointing in screening, and unacceptable on one camera. None of these verdicts is wrong; each answers a different question.

What to notice

Balanced benchmark: accuracy 0.92. Screening at 5%: precision (0.90 × 0.05)/(0.045 + 0.06 × 0.95) ≈ 0.44 — more than half of referrals are false alarms. Rural camera: sensitivity 0.78 (Math Box 148.1).

A single headline number would hide both the precision problem and the subgroup failure. Evaluation is the construction of a picture adequate to a decision: which population, which operating point, which subgroups, which harms (Chapters 21, 26, 148, 158).
