Game-tree search: minimax and the explosion

To choose a move, assume the opponent will reply as well as possible, and pick the move whose worst case is best. It works — until the tree grows. Each extra level multiplies the positions by the branching factor.

What to notice

Move A contains the best outcome (+3) but is worse: the opponent answers with −2. B guarantees +1, so minimax plays B. Chess, b ≈ 35: 1.5 million positions at depth 4, 2.8 × 10¹⁵ at depth 10 (Math Boxes 4.1, 5.1).

Go has b ≈ 250. No speed-up conquers b^d: every extra level costs a factor of b. Progress came from knowledge — pruning, evaluation functions, and eventually learned value networks that let search look at far fewer positions (Chapters 4, 5, 7, 64).
