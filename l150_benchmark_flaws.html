Two benchmark design flaws, in numbers

Wrong reference answers cap what a perfect model can score and reward models that share the mistakes. Multiple choice gives free points for guessing. Both distort the numbers most near chance and near the ceiling — where comparisons are usually made.

What to notice

With 5% wrong labels, a perfect model scores 95%. On four-option questions guessing scores 25%, so an observed 40% means only (0.40 − 0.25)/(1 − 0.25) = 20% genuinely known (Math Box 150.1).

Slide “learned the mistakes” up: a model that absorbed the benchmark’s errors — for instance through contamination — scores higher than a correct one. Near the top, gains of a point or two can be label noise or memorization rather than improvement (Chapters 149–151).
