Why logarithms turn products into sums

The probability a model gives a whole dataset is a product of many numbers below one. Multiply enough of them and the computer runs out of digits. Their logarithms simply add.

What to notice

0.9 × 0.5 × 0.2 = 0.09; ln 0.09 = −2.408 = ln 0.9 + ln 0.5 + ln 0.2 (Math Box 22.1).

At probability 0.5 per example the product underflows to exactly zero after about 1,075 examples — every model then looks equally bad. The log-sum never does. That is why every loss in this book is a sum of logs: cross-entropy, negative log-likelihood, perplexity (Chapters 22, 28, 33).
