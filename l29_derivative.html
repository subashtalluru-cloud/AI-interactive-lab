A derivative tells you which way to move

One weight, one example: ŷ = w·2, target 6, loss L(w) = (2w − 6)². The derivative is the slope of the loss curve; its sign says which way lowers the loss and its size says how steeply.

What to notice

At w = 1 the slope is −16: move up. At w = 4 it is +8: move down. At w = 3 it is 0 — the minimum, where 3 × 2 = 6 fits exactly (Math Box 29.1).

With η = 0.05 each step moves w by 0.4 × its distance to 3 — steady convergence. Above η = 0.25 the steps overshoot by more than they correct and w diverges: the same threshold, 2 ÷ curvature, as the gradient-descent lab (curvature here is 8). Backpropagation is this, for millions of weights at once (Chapters 29–31, 39).
