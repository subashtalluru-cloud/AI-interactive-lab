The dot product as an alignment score

Drag the tips of the two arrows. The dot product combines how long they are with how closely they point the same way; the cosine keeps only the direction. Attention, semantic search and retrieval are built from this one number.

What to notice

Rotate b around a without changing its length: the dot product traces a cosine — largest when aligned, zero at a right angle, negative when opposed. Then stretch b while keeping its direction: the dot product grows but the cosine does not move.

That is why retrieval systems usually compare cosines (or normalize embeddings first): a long document vector should not win just by being long. Attention (Chapter 68) uses raw dot products, scaled by √d, which lets magnitude carry information too.
