Why models guess, and how scoring changes it

A model knows the answers to some questions and is unsure about the rest. If a wrong answer costs nothing, guessing always scores at least as well as saying “I don’t know”. Set the scoring rule and see which behaviour wins.

What to notice

With no penalty, always guessing scores 76 against 70 for abstaining — and produces 24 confident errors. A penalty of 1 per wrong answer flips it: 52 against 70.

The break-even point is simple: guessing pays only when the chance of being right exceeds penalty ÷ (1 + penalty). With penalty 1 that is 50%; with penalty 3 it is 75%. A model optimized against a benchmark that never penalizes errors is being trained to bluff. In clinical use a confident wrong answer usually costs far more than “I don’t know — check the formulary” (Chapters 136, 148, 152).
