A normalizing flow in one dimension

A flow turns simple noise into data through invertible steps. Because each step can be undone, the density of any data point can be computed exactly: map it back to noise, read the simple density there, and correct for how much the step stretched space.

What to notice

x = 2z + 1 at x = 3: invert to z = 1, p_z(1) = 0.242, divide by the stretch 2 → 0.121 — exactly the density of N(1, 2²) at 3 (Math Box 114.1).

Add the bend: the density becomes non-Gaussian, yet it is still exact, because the bend is invertible and its slope 1 + k·(1 − tanh²) is known. Real flows stack many such steps in many dimensions; the price of exactness is that every step must stay invertible with a cheap slope (the determinant of its Jacobian) (Chapter 114).
