Understanding Artificial Intelligence Chapters 21, 158 · Lab L158.1

One model, two groups, several kinds of fair

A screening model scores patients from two groups of 1,000. Its scores separate disease less well in Group B, and disease is commoner there. Move the thresholds and the prevalences and see which kinds of equality can hold at the same time.

What to notice

With one shared threshold the model catches 90% of disease in Group A but only 75% in Group B — the group with more disease. The overall sensitivity (80%) hides the gap entirely.

“Equalize sensitivity” lowers B’s threshold until both catch the same share of disease; the cost is more false alarms in B, so specificity and PPV now differ. “Equalize PPV” makes a referral mean the same thing in both groups, and sensitivity diverges again. When prevalence differs and the model is imperfect, sensitivity, specificity and PPV cannot all be equal at once (Chapter 158) — choosing which to equalize is a clinical and ethical decision, not a technical one. Improving separation in Group B (better data, better model) is the only move that helps everywhere.
