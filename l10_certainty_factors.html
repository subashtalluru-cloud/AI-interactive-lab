Certainty factors: how expert systems combined evidence

MYCIN-era expert systems combined rules that supported the same conclusion with a simple formula: each new piece of evidence closes a fraction of the remaining gap to certainty. Add rules and see how confidence grows — and why the method was eventually replaced by probability.

What to notice

0.6 and 0.5 combine to 0.6 + 0.5 × 0.4 = 0.8; a third rule of 0.5 gives 0.9 (Math Box 10.1).

Press “add the same evidence again”: the formula treats a restatement of the same finding as independent support and raises confidence anyway. Real evidence is often correlated. Bayes’ theorem (Chapter 26) handles this properly through conditional probabilities — one of the reasons the field moved from hand-built certainty calculi to probability and then to learning from data (Chapters 10–12).
