Buying accuracy with inference compute

A reasoning model can spend more tokens thinking, sample several traces and vote, or keep any trace a verifier accepts. Each raises accuracy — at a price in time, compute and energy for every single answer.

What to notice

One 5,000-token trace on a 70B model costs about 7 × 10¹⁴ FLOPs and 100 seconds. Eight traces at 60% each, majority-voted, reach about 0.71; with a verifier, about 0.999 (Math Box 164.1).

Set per-trace accuracy below 0.5 and voting makes things worse: the majority converges on the wrong answer. Voting amplifies whatever the model usually does; a verifier can rescue a rare right answer. And the independence assumption is generous — real traces from one model make correlated mistakes, so real gains are smaller (Chapters 140, 164, 166).
