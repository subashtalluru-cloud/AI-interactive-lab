Model collapse in miniature

IOP readings drawn from Normal(16, 3²). Each generation fits a normal distribution to 10 samples produced by the previous generation’s model. With replacement, the spread drains away; when real data are kept and synthetic data accumulate alongside them, it does not.

What to notice

With 10 samples and replacement, each generation’s expected variance is 0.9 × the last; after 20 generations 0.9²⁰ ≈ 0.12 of the original (0.9²¹ ≈ 0.11 if generation 0’s own fit to the real data is counted, as the readout does) — standard deviation from 3 to about 1 mmHg — while the centre wanders.

The 0.12 is an average over many lineages. Each generation multiplies the variance by a random factor, and random factors compound unevenly: a few lineages keep much of their spread while the typical one shrinks far more, often below 1 mmHg. Run several batches and compare the median with the expectation.

Switch to accumulation: the real samples never leave the training set, and the spread stays near 3. This mirrors Shumailov et al. (2024) and the follow-up work on accumulating data (Chapter 170). What a model finds improbable, it under-produces — the rare, the unusual, the minority case, often exactly what matters in medicine.
