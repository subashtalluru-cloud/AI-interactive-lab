Why one preference number blurs trade-offs

Labellers rate three answers to a borderline medication question on helpfulness and harmlessness. Collapse the two into one weighted score and the ranking can hide exactly the conflict that mattered.

What to notice

A (9, 2) averages 5.5, B (1, 9) averages 5.0, C (7, 9) averages 8.0 (Math Box 96.1). C wins at almost any weight: it is good on both.

Remove C and the choice flips between A (states a dangerous quantity) and B (refuses outright) at weight 0.53 — and at an equal weighting the dangerous answer narrowly wins. A single number cannot express “this must never fall below a floor”. That is why post-training uses separate criteria, constitutions and hard rules alongside scalar rewards (Chapters 93, 96).
