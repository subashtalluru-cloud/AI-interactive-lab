Extrapolating agent time horizons

METR measures an agent’s “time horizon”: the length of task, in human working time, it completes with 50% success. The horizon has grown roughly exponentially. Extrapolating a doubling trend is simple arithmetic — and very sensitive to the doubling time you assume.

What to notice

From 12 hours: with 4.3-month doubling, 96 hours after 12.9 months; with 7-month doubling, about 43 hours (Math Box 173.1). The band widens every month.

These are extrapolations, not forecasts [SPECULATIVE]. The horizon is read off a curve fitted to few long tasks, so small changes in success on those tasks move it a lot; benchmarks near their ceiling measure less well; and a 50% success rate is far from the reliability needed for unsupervised work, which the 80% line shows. Trends of this kind can also bend — the S-curves of Chapter 180.
