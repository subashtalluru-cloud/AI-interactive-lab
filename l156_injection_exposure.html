Rare per document, near-certain per year

An assistant that reads outside documents will sometimes meet one carrying hidden instructions. A tiny chance per document, multiplied over months of routine use, becomes close to a certainty — which is why defences must limit the damage of a successful injection, not only its likelihood.

What to notice

With the book’s numbers — 50 documents a day, 5% carrying an attempt, 1% of attempts succeeding — the per-document risk is 0.0005, about 2.5% per day, and about 99.8% over a year of working days.

Cutting the success rate tenfold still leaves roughly a 46% yearly chance — close to a coin toss — and more than half a successful injection expected each year. That is why Chapter 156 argues for containment: fewer tools, confirmation before consequential actions, separating instructions from content, and least-privilege access, so that the injection that eventually succeeds can do little harm. The same arithmetic governs rare adverse events in any high-volume process.
