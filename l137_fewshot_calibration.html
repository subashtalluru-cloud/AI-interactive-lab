Detecting and correcting bias from few-shot examples

A few-shot prompt whose examples are mostly “normal” tilts the model toward “normal” even when the input carries no information. Measure that tilt with a content-free probe, then divide it out.

What to notice

Content-free 0.70/0.30; test 0.60/0.40. Divide: 0.60/0.70 = 0.857 and 0.40/0.30 = 1.333; renormalize to 0.39/0.61. The answer flips to “abnormal” (Math Box 137.1).

The test sentence scored less normal than an empty input — which is evidence of abnormality once the prompt’s own lean is removed. Balancing and shuffling the examples reduces the lean at source; this correction is a simplified version of contextual calibration (Zhao et al.). Either way, measure on a held-out set before trusting it (Chapters 26, 137).
