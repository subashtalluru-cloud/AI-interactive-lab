Where did each part of the answer come from?

A system answer mixes claims from retrieved documents, tool results, stored memory and the model’s own synthesis — and, if uncited, its training. Each source fails differently and must be checked differently. Mark each claim and see what verification it needs.

What to notice

In the book’s example, five claims come from five different layers: a formulary chunk, another chunk, a record query, a stored note, and the model’s synthesis — each with its own check (Math Box 133.1).

Mark a retrieved claim as “uncited” and it becomes parametric knowledge: unverifiable by lookup, and the most likely home of confident errors. Systems that label provenance per claim make review fast; systems that blend everything into fluent prose make it slow (Chapters 124–133).
