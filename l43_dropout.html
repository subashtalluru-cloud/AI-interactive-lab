Dropout: training many thinned networks at once

At every training step, each hidden unit is switched off with probability p; the survivors are scaled up by 1/(1 − p) so the next layer receives the same input on average. No unit can rely on any particular partner.

What to notice

The book’s masks at p = 0.5: keep units 1 and 4 → (1.6, 0, 0, 4.0); keep units 2 and 3 → (0, 2.4, 0.8, 0). The expected value of unit 1 is 0.5 × 1.6 + 0.5 × 0 = 0.8, its original value (Math Box 43.1).

The average is right, but any single step is noisy — raise p and the noise grows. That noise is the regularizer: each step trains a different sub-network, and at test time dropout is switched off and the full network behaves roughly like their average, the ensemble effect of Chapter 17 for almost no cost. Forgetting to switch it off at inference (model.eval()) gives random, degraded predictions.
