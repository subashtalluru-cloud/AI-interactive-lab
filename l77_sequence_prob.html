Scoring a sentence, one token at a time

A language model assigns a sentence a probability by multiplying the probability of each token given the ones before it. Logs turn the product into a sum; the average surprise per token becomes perplexity.

What to notice

0.001 × 0.02 × 0.9 = 0.000018; log-probability −6.91 − 3.91 − 0.105 = −10.92; average 3.64 nats per token; perplexity ≈ 38 (Math Box 77.1).

Nearly all the cost lies in the first two words, where many continuations were plausible; once “raised intraocular” is present, “pressure” is almost free. A good model is confident where language is predictable and appropriately uncertain where it is not — and pretraining minimizes exactly this sum over trillions of tokens (Chapters 33, 77, 83). Sliders are on a log₁₀ scale.
