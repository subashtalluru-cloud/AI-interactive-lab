The cost of a silent guess

When a question has two readings, a model that silently picks the more common one is wrong for everyone who meant the other. Compare guessing, asking, answering both, and stating the assumption.

What to notice

70% mean A; 95% accurate when interpreted right: silent guessing gives 0.7 × 0.95 ≈ 0.67; asking first gives about 0.95, at the cost of a turn (Math Box 143.1).

Stating the assumption is often the best compromise: the answer arrives at once, and users who meant B can see it was not what they asked. In clinical use the 30% minority may be exactly the patients who matter most (Chapters 135, 143).
