Quantization: fewer bits per weight

Store each weight as a small integer times one shared scale. Eight bits give 255 levels; four bits give 15. Fewer levels means a coarser grid — and small weights can fall to zero.

What to notice

At 8 bits the scale is 0.01 and the largest error is 0.004. At 4 bits the scale is about 0.181, errors reach 0.06, and the weight 0.051 becomes exactly 0 — reproducing Math Box 100.1.

Tick “outlier”. One large weight stretches the scale for everyone, so the ordinary weights are squeezed onto a handful of levels. This is why practical schemes quantize in small groups with their own scales, or keep outlier channels at higher precision (Chapter 100). Memory, however, falls in proportion to bits: a 7-billion-parameter model needs about 14 GB at 16 bits and about 3.5 GB at 4.
