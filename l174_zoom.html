Does zooming help? Lesions and resolution

A vision encoder sees a fixed-size image. Downscale a high-resolution fundus to fit and a small lesion shrinks below a patch. Cropping and re-encoding a region restores it — if the model looks in the right place.

What to notice

3,000 px → 336 px: a 30-pixel microaneurysm becomes about 3.3 pixels, far below one 14-pixel patch. A 600-pixel crop re-encoded at 336 makes it about 17 pixels; covering the whole image this way needs about 25 crops, ~14,400 tokens (Math Box 174.1).

A model that does not suspect a lesion may never zoom to it. For screening, specialist high-resolution models or systems that tile the whole image systematically remain safer than a general model deciding where to look (Chapters 48, 118, 174).
