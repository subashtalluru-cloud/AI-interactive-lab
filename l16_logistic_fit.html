Fitting a logistic curve to the ten eyes

Logistic regression turns a straight line z = w·IOP + b into a probability with the sigmoid. Cross-entropy measures how much probability the curve gave to what actually happened; gradient descent adjusts w and b to raise it.

What to notice

The book’s candidate w = 0.5, b = −11 puts the boundary at IOP 22 and gives probabilities 0.018 … 0.982, mean loss about 0.467 (Chapter 16).

The gradient is Σ(p − y)·x / n and Σ(p − y) / n — prediction minus truth, the same “error times input” pattern as linear regression and backpropagation (Chapters 15, 16, 31). The two overlapping eyes (IOP 19 progressed, 24 did not) keep the loss from reaching zero: no single threshold separates them, so the fitted curve stays gentle rather than becoming a step.
