Compute-bound or memory-bound?

An accelerator can do a fixed number of operations per byte it fetches from memory. Work that does more arithmetic per byte than that is limited by compute; work that does less waits on memory. The same model is one in training and the other in decoding.

What to notice

3 × 10¹⁴ FLOP/s ÷ 2 × 10¹² bytes/s = 150 FLOPs per byte. A 4,096 × 4,096 matrix–matrix product: ~1,370 FLOPs/byte (compute-bound). The same matrix times one vector: ~1 FLOP/byte (memory-bound) (Math Box 104.1).

Batching turns matrix–vector products into matrix–matrix products and climbs the roof; quantization moves fewer bytes. Much of inference engineering is about moving an operation from the slope to the flat (Chapters 88, 100, 104, 105).
