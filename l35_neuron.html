What one neuron does to the plane

A neuron computes σ(w₁x₁ + w₂x₂ + b). Over every possible input it paints the plane: a straight boundary where the output is 0.5, rising smoothly on one side. The weights choose the direction; the bias slides the line; the overall scale sets how sharp it is.

What to notice

Change w₁ and w₂ and the line rotates — it is always at right angles to the weight vector. Change b and the line slides without turning. Scale everything up and the line stays put while the transition sharpens towards a hard step: the perceptron of Chapter 34 is the sigmoid neuron at infinite scale.

A single neuron can only draw one straight line — this is why XOR needs a hidden layer (Chapter 36). Logistic regression (Chapter 16) is exactly this picture with a sigmoid; its weights are learned rather than set by hand. With ReLU the flat side is exactly 0 and the other side rises without limit — a detector that says “how much”, not “how likely”.
