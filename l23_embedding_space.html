Meaning as direction: a toy embedding space

Fourteen ophthalmic terms placed as vectors in two dimensions. Click a term to rank all the others by cosine similarity; type a query made of known terms to see retrieval as nearest-direction search.

The coordinates are hand-placed for teaching, standing in for a 2-D view of a real learned space; real embeddings have hundreds to thousands of dimensions, and a 2-D picture of them always distorts some distances.

    What to notice

    Click “glaucoma”: ocular hypertension, trabeculectomy and optic disc cupping come first (cosines 0.97–0.99), then the drugs timolol and latanoprost, while cataract surgery and uveitis terms score negative — they share a direction, not a spelling. Nobody wrote the rule; in a real model the geometry comes from which words appear in similar contexts (Chapter 57).

    Type “cataract drops”: the query vector is the average of its parts, and it lands between two clusters. Semantic search returns whatever lies nearest that blended direction — sometimes exactly what was meant, sometimes a compromise that matches neither. This is one reason retrieval systems add keyword matching and re-ranking (Chapters 126–127).
