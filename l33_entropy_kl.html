Entropy, cross-entropy and KL divergence

The truth P and a model Q are distributions over three outcomes. Entropy is the unavoidable surprise of P itself; cross-entropy is the surprise you actually experience when you use Q; the gap between them — KL divergence — is the price of the model being wrong.

What to notice

The book’s case: P = (0.5, 0.5), Q = (0.9, 0.1): H(P) = 0.693, H(P, Q) = 1.204, KL = 0.511 nats (Math Box 33.1).

Make P one-hot (all on A): H(P) becomes 0 and cross-entropy is just −ln Q(A) — the training loss of Chapters 16 and 28. Push Q toward zero on an outcome P says can happen, and KL shoots up: confident dismissal of something real is punished hardest. Swap the direction and KL(Q‖P) gives a different number — which direction a method uses (RLHF, VAEs, distillation) shapes what the model is pushed to do (Chapters 94, 101, 110).
