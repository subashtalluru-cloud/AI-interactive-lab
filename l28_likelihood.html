Maximum likelihood, one coin toss at a time

For each possible value of p, the chance of heads, ask: how probable would the tosses we actually saw have been? That function of p is the likelihood. Its peak is the maximum-likelihood estimate — and its width shows how sure the data let us be.

What to notice

After 7 heads in 10 tosses the curve peaks at p = 0.7 but is wide — values from about 0.4 to 0.9 remain plausible. After 70 in 100 it peaks at the same place but is about three times narrower: more data, sharper belief.

Switch to negative log-likelihood: the peak becomes a minimum — this is the curve training descends. For a classifier the same quantity is cross-entropy; for a language model, the average −ln P(next token). “Minimize the loss” and “find the parameters under which the data were most probable” are one instruction (Math Box 28.1).
