Same content, different token budgets

A minute of speech, an image, a scan volume: each can be represented as text, as encoder frames, or as tokens — at very different costs, and keeping very different information.

What to notice

One minute of speech: about 200 transcript tokens against about 3,000 encoder frames. One OCT volume of 128 B-scans at 512 × 512 with 16-pixel patches: 131,072 patch tokens (Math Box 121.1).

The transcript is 15× cheaper but discards tone, hesitation, breathlessness, slurred speech — sometimes the clinical sign itself. Volumes exceed many context windows, which is why 3-D imaging usually needs specialist encoders or selective slices (Chapters 121–123).
