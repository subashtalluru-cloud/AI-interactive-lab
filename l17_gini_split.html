How a decision tree chooses its question

The ten eyes of Chapter 11: IOP and whether glaucoma progressed. A tree asks “is IOP ≤ t?” and picks the threshold that makes the two resulting groups as pure as possible, measured by Gini impurity.

What to notice

Split at 18.5: left {14, 16, 18} all non-progressors (Gini 0); right Gini 20/49 ≈ 0.408; weighted 0.286; gain 0.214. Split at 21.5: both sides 0.32, gain 0.18. Both splits make 2 errors, but Gini prefers the one that creates a pure group (Math Box 17.1). The split at 24.5 ties with 18.5 — it creates a pure group of three progressors — and the lab, like most tree software, keeps the first threshold it finds.

Switch the measure to “error rate”: 18.5 and 21.5 tie, because error rate cannot see purity. Gini and entropy can, which is why trees use them. Keep splitting and every eye ends up alone in a leaf — perfect on these ten, useless on the next ten. That memorization is why single trees are pruned and why forests average many (Chapter 17).
