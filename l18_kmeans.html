k-means, one step at a time

Two moves, repeated: assign every point to its nearest centre, then move each centre to the mean of its points. Each move can only lower the total squared distance J, so the process settles — though not always in the best place.

What to notice

The book’s run: from centres 1 and 2, the first update moves the second centre to 7.6; the next gives 2 and 11; then nothing changes. J = 4 (Math Box 18.1).

On the three-cluster data, press “New random start” a few times with k = 3: sometimes two centres share one cluster and one centre straddles two — a local minimum with larger J. That is why k-means is run several times. On elongated clusters it cuts across the natural groups, because it assumes round ones. And k is your choice: the algorithm will happily return six clusters of anything. Whether a cluster is a disease subtype or a scanner artefact is a scientific question (Chapter 18).
