The KL leash: reward against drift

RLHF rewards responses the reward model likes, minus a penalty for how far the policy’s token probabilities have moved from the reference model’s. The penalty strength β decides whether a reward-model quirk can win.

What to notice

β = 0.1: Response 1 gets 2.0 − 0.1 × 0.6 = 1.94; Response 2, which the reward model scores higher (2.5) but the reference finds very unlikely (log-ratios sum 7.0), gets 2.5 − 0.7 = 1.80. The natural answer wins (Math Box 94.1).

The crossover here is β ≈ 0.078: below it, the policy is free to chase the reward model’s taste for odd phrasing. Too large a β and the policy barely changes from the reference at all. DPO builds the same trade-off directly into its loss, with β playing the same role (Chapters 93–95).
