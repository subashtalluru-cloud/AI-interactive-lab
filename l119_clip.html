Contrastive learning on a batch of three

CLIP-style training scores every image against every caption in a batch. Each row and each column becomes a small classification problem: the matching pair should win, and every other item is a negative example.

Cosine similarities (edit any cell). Rows: (1) fundus with disc haemorrhage, (2) OCT with macular fluid, (3) slit-lamp cataract. Columns: their captions.

What to notice

With the book’s similarities and τ = 0.5: row losses 0.37, 0.51, 0.62 (mean 0.50), column mean 0.49, total ≈ 0.50. Image 3 is the weakest because it sits close to caption 2.

Press “one training step” repeatedly: correct pairs are pulled together and the strongest wrong pair is pushed apart first. Lowering τ sharpens every softmax — small similarity differences become large probability differences. Larger batches give each item more negatives, which is why CLIP trained with 32,768 pairs per batch (Chapter 119).
