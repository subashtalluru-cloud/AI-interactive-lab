Correlation, covariance and the regression slope

Drag the points. Covariance asks whether x and y tend to sit above (or below) their means together; correlation rescales that to −1…+1; and the least-squares slope is simply covariance divided by the variance of x.

What to notice

The book’s data (1,2), (2,3), (3,5), (4,6): products 3, 0.5, 0.5, 3; Cov = 1.75; Var(x) = 1.25; slope 1.75/1.25 = 1.4; r ≈ 0.99 (Math Box 27.1).

The U-shape has a perfect relationship and r ≈ 0: correlation measures only straight-line association. One outlier can manufacture or destroy a correlation. And none of it says which way causation runs, or whether it runs at all.
