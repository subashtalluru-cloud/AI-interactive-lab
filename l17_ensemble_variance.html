Why averaging many models helps — and where it stops

Average B models, each with prediction variance σ². If their errors were independent, the variance would fall as σ²/B. But models trained on similar data make similar mistakes, and shared mistakes do not average away.

What to notice

With σ² = 1: independent models, B = 100 → 0.01. At ρ = 0.5, B = 100 → 0.505, and even infinitely many models only reach 0.5 (Math Box 17.2).

This is why random forests work hard to decorrelate their trees — each is trained on a bootstrap sample and considers a random subset of features at every split. Lowering ρ helps more than adding trees. The same arithmetic governs majority voting by several AI graders (Chapter 132) and repeated samples from one language model (Chapter 164): voters who share blind spots vote together.
