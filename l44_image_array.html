An image is a grid of numbers

To a network, a fundus photograph is three grids of brightness values, one per colour. Zoom in until the pixels show, read their numbers, and count what a fully connected layer would face.

What to notice

A 512 × 512 colour image is 786,432 numbers. A fully connected layer with just 1,000 units would need about 786 million weights for its first layer alone.

Neighbouring pixels are nearly equal, and a vessel looks the same wherever it runs. Convolutions exploit both facts: small kernels, reused everywhere (Chapters 44–46). The green channel usually carries the most vessel contrast in fundus images — try it.
