PPO: improve, but not by too much at once

The objective is min(r·A, clip(r, 1−ε, 1+ε)·A), where r is how much the update has changed an action’s probability and A is how much better than expected that action turned out. Where the curve goes flat, the gradient is zero and the update stops pushing.

What to notice

For a good action (A > 0), the objective rises with r only until 1+ε, then goes flat: no reward for making it more than about 20% likelier in one step. For a bad action (A < 0), it goes flat below 1−ε: no reward for suppressing it by more than about 20%.

The minimum makes the rule one-sided. If an update has accidentally moved the wrong way — a good action made less likely, or a bad one more likely — the unclipped term is the smaller one and the full gradient pulls it back. PPO limits over-reaction to good news, never correction of mistakes. The same caution is what keeps RLHF (Chapter 93) from chasing one reward-model quirk too far in a single step.
