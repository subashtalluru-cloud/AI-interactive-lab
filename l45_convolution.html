Convolution: one small kernel, slid everywhere

A convolution slides a small grid of weights over the image. At each position it takes a dot product with the patch underneath — a question asked everywhere at once: “is there a dark-to-bright edge here?”, “is this spot brighter than its surroundings?”.

What to notice

The book’s image and 2 × 2 kernel give a 3 × 3 feature map: 0, 2, 0 in every row — strong response only where the window straddles the edge (Math Box 45.1).

On the fundus, the vertical-edge kernel lights up the left and right borders of vessels and disc; the Laplacian “spot detector” outlines the macula, the disc and every vessel — at this kernel size it responds wherever brightness changes abruptly, so it rings the borders rather than filling the spots. A CNN learns hundreds of such kernels from data rather than having them designed (Chapters 45–47), and stacks them so later layers detect combinations: vessel crossings, haemorrhages, cupping.
