Is model A really better than model B?

Two models, true accuracies differing by a small amount, each tested on n cases. Run the comparison many times and count how often the test set even gets the ranking right — and how often it would be declared significant.

What to notice

At 85% accuracy, a true 2-point advantage needs about 5,000 cases per model to be detected reliably. With 300 cases the observed difference ranges widely around the truth, B frequently appears better, and only differences of about 8 points can be reliably detected.

Raise the paired-agreement slider: when both models are scored on the same cases and tend to succeed and fail together, their difference is much less noisy (Chapter 149). This is why “model A outperformed model B” on a few hundred cases is often not a reliable claim — and why expert-graded evaluations, where each case costs specialist time, lag behind development (Chapter 163).
