The average hides the weakest point

A red team runs 500 jailbreak attempts across ten technique families. The overall success rate looks low. But an attacker does not pick families at random — they use whatever works best, and they try more than once.

What to notice

The book’s numbers: 35 of 500 overall (7%), but encoding attacks succeed 24% of the time; five encoding attempts give 1 − 0.76⁵ ≈ 75% chance of at least one success (Math Box 157.1).

Report worst-family and repeated-attempt figures, not just the mean. Note too how wide each family’s interval is at 50 attempts: 12/50 is compatible with anything from about 14% to 37%. The same arithmetic as prompt injection (Chapter 156) and red teaming (Chapter 161): what matters is what a persistent adversary achieves.
