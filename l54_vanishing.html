Why gradients vanish or explode across time

A one-number recurrent network: h_t = tanh(w_h · h_{t−1} + x_t), with a single event x₁ = 1 at step 1. Backpropagation multiplies one factor per step. Watch what repeated multiplication does to the signal that says “step 1 mattered”.

What to notice

At w_h = 0.5 the signal from step 1 falls to about 5% after four steps and to around 10⁻¹⁵ after fifty: the network is told nothing about long-range causes, so it never learns them.

Push w_h above 1 with the tanh removed and the line climbs instead — an explosion: 49 backward factors of 1.5 give about 4 × 10⁸. With the tanh on, large weights saturate the states, the tanh slopes fall toward zero, and the gradient vanishes anyway: saturation is another route to the same failure.

Tick “residual path”. Each factor becomes 1 + (small term), so the product stays of order one (about 3 at w_h = 0.5 over fifty steps) instead of collapsing to 10⁻¹⁵. This is the same idea as the LSTM’s cell state (Chapter 55), the ResNet skip (Chapter 50) and the transformer’s residual stream (Chapter 73).
